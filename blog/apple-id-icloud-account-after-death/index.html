What Happens to an Apple ID and iCloud When Someone Dies?

By Departed Digital · 8 min read · Updated August 2026

An Apple ID is rarely just an account. It usually holds the photo library, the messages, the notes, and the backups of a person's whole phone. When someone dies, their Apple ID becomes one of the most important digital accounts to deal with, and also one of the strictest. Apple takes privacy seriously, which protects your loved one, but it also means the family cannot simply log in and take what they need.

This guide explains how Apple handles a death, what happens to the iCloud data, and the exact routes available depending on whether your loved one set up a Legacy Contact. It applies wherever you are in the world, with small differences we point out along the way.

Read This Before You Do Anything Else

One warning matters more than everything else in this guide: closing an Apple ID permanently deletes the iCloud data attached to it. That includes the photo library, iCloud Drive files, notes, and device backups. Once Apple processes an account deletion, that material cannot be recovered.

So before you ask Apple to close anything, make sure the family has preserved what it wants to keep. If a family member knows the passcode of the person's iPhone or iPad, photos and files can often be saved directly from the device itself. Do not wipe, trade in, or factory reset any Apple device until you are certain the data on it is safe.

Route One: A Legacy Contact Was Set Up

Since iOS 15.2, Apple has offered a feature called Digital Legacy. It lets a person nominate one or more Legacy Contacts who can access their account data after they die. When someone sets this up, each Legacy Contact receives an access key, either as a printed document or stored digitally in the contact's own Apple account.

If your loved one set this up, you have the smoothest path available on any major platform:

1
Find the access key

Check the Legacy Contact's own iPhone under Settings, then their name, then Sign-In & Security and Legacy Contact. A printed copy may also be with the person's will or important papers. The key contains a QR code and a long code beginning with a series of letters and numbers.

2
Request access from Apple

Go to Apple's Digital Legacy request page (search "Apple Digital Legacy request" or visit digital-legacy.apple.com). You will need the access key and a copy of the death certificate.

3
Receive a legacy account

Once Apple approves the request, the Legacy Contact gets a special Apple ID to view and download the person's photos, messages, notes, files, and backups. Access lasts for a limited period, typically three years, after which the account is deleted, so download what the family wants to keep early.

Note what Digital Legacy does not include: it does not give access to the person's iCloud Keychain passwords or payment information, and it does not transfer purchased films, music, or subscriptions.

Route Two: No Legacy Contact Was Set Up

Most people never set up a Legacy Contact. In that case the family's options depend on what you need.

If you only need the account closed

Any close family member or the person handling the estate can ask Apple to close a deceased person's account. Contact Apple Support and ask for help with a deceased customer's account, and be ready to upload the death certificate. Apple reviews the documents and permanently deletes the Apple ID and its data. There is no fee, and you do not need a court document just to close the account in most cases.

If you need the data without an access key

This is the hard road. Apple requires a court order that names the requester as the rightful inheritor or personal representative of the deceased person's digital assets. In the UK this usually means an order from a court, not just a grant of probate. In the US it means a court order meeting the requirements Apple sets out in its support pages. Many families decide the material on the physical devices is enough, because obtaining a court order takes months and usually needs a solicitor or attorney.

Unlocking Devices: Activation Lock

Even after the account question is settled, families often find they cannot reuse or sell the person's iPhone, iPad, or Mac because Activation Lock is still tied to the deceased person's Apple ID. Apple can remove Activation Lock for a family if you provide the death certificate and proof that the device belonged to the person, such as the original receipt or details from the estate. Ask Apple Support for Activation Lock removal for a deceased customer. Do not pay third-party unlocking services; Apple's own route is free and legitimate.

Subscriptions and Payments

An Apple ID often carries active subscriptions: iCloud+ storage, Apple Music, Apple TV+, and app subscriptions, all charging a card monthly. Closing the account stops these. If the family needs the account kept open for a while, for example to preserve photos until a decision is made, contact the bank about stopping the payments rather than letting them run. Be aware that if iCloud storage payments stop while the account remains open, Apple may reduce the storage and data over the limit can eventually be at risk, which is another reason to preserve what matters early.

The Short Version

  • Preserve first. Save photos and files from the person's devices before asking Apple to close anything.
  • Check for a Legacy Contact. It is the smoothest route to the data and many families do not realise it was set up.
  • Closing the account is straightforward with a death certificate via Apple Support.
  • Recovering data without an access key needs a court order, which is slow and usually needs legal help.
  • Ask Apple to remove Activation Lock before selling or reusing devices.

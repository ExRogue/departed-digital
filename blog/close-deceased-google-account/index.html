How to Close a Deceased Person's Google Account

By Departed Digital · 7 min read · Updated September 2026

A Google account is never just email. The same login holds Gmail, the photo library in Google Photos, files in Drive, a YouTube channel, saved passwords in Chrome, and often the person's phone backups. That makes it one of the most consequential accounts to deal with after a death, and the one where acting too fast destroys the most.

Google's process is more centralised than Meta's: one request flow covers everything. This guide explains it for families anywhere in the world.

Before Anything: What Deletion Destroys

Closing a Google account permanently deletes the Gmail history, every photo in Google Photos, everything in Drive, and any YouTube channel, including monetised ones. None of it can be recovered afterwards. If there is any chance the family wants the photos, deal with preservation first and closure second. Nothing bad happens if the account stays open for another few months while you decide.

Check for Inactive Account Manager

Google's planning feature, Inactive Account Manager, lets a person decide in advance what happens when their account goes quiet: after a chosen period of inactivity, Google notifies trusted contacts and can grant them access to selected data, or delete the account automatically.

If your loved one set this up, the nominated contact will receive an email from Google when the inactivity period passes, with a link to download whatever was shared. You cannot trigger it early, but it is worth knowing whether it exists, because it may quietly resolve the data question on its own. If the person mentioned a trusted contact, check that inbox, including spam.

Google's Deceased User Request

Everything else goes through one flow: search "Google deceased user request" or go to Google's Account Help and choose Submit a request regarding a deceased user's account. It offers three options, and you can submit more than one:

  • Close the account. The simplest request. Immediate family or the estate's representative submit the death certificate and their own ID. Google reviews and closes the account.
  • Request funds from the account. Where the person had money with Google, such as AdSense or YouTube earnings or a Google Play balance, the estate can request it. Expect to provide the estate paperwork for your country: a grant of probate or letters of administration in the UK, Ireland, Australia, or New Zealand, or letters testamentary in the US.
  • Request data from the account. The hardest route. Google reviews these case by case and, for anything substantial, requires a court order from your jurisdiction directing the disclosure. Many families conclude the photos on the person's actual phone or computer are the more practical source.
1
Gather the documents

The death certificate, your own government ID, and, for funds or data requests, the estate document naming you. Clear photos or scans are fine, and Google accepts them digitally through the form.

2
Submit the form with the person's Gmail address

You need the exact address. If you only know part of it, check the family's own email history for messages from the person.

3
Reply to Google's follow-up email

Google responds by email, sometimes weeks later, occasionally asking for notarised or translated documents for cross-border cases. Reply in the same thread and keep copies of everything.

What Stops When the Account Closes

Closure has side effects families do not always expect. Anything else signed in with that Google login, from streaming services to food delivery, loses its login route. Subscriptions billed through Google Play stop. A shared family calendar or shared Drive folders owned by the person disappear for everyone. If the household relies on any of those, note them before closing, and move ownership of shared files first where possible.

Common Problems

Google asks for documents you cannot get

For a simple closure, push back politely in the email thread: closure needs proof of death and identity, not a court order. Court orders relate to data disclosure. Restating your request narrowly, "close the account only", often unblocks it.

The account keeps sending calendar invites or sync messages

That is automation, not activity. It stops when the account closes, and it is not a sign anyone has access.

An Android phone is locked to the account

Factory-reset protection ties Android devices to the Google login. Closure does not remove it. If the family wants to reuse the phone, deal with the device before closing the account, or contact the manufacturer with proof of ownership and the death certificate.

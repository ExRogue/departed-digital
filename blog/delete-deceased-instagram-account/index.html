How to Remove a Deceased Person's Instagram Account

By Departed Digital · 6 min read · Updated September 2026

An Instagram profile is often the most personal account a person leaves behind: years of photos, stories, and messages. It is also the account most visible to a younger circle of friends, who keep encountering it long after the funeral. Instagram, owned by Meta, offers families the same two outcomes as Facebook, memorialisation or removal, but through its own forms and with its own quirks.

This guide works wherever you are; only the document proving your authority differs by country.

Save the Photos First

Removal is permanent, and Instagram does not hand families an archive afterwards. Before you request anything:

  • Save the public photos and videos directly from the profile, or screenshot them.
  • If a family member knows the login, use Instagram's own Download your information tool in settings to export everything first. Meta's terms technically prohibit logging in to another person's account, so where possible do this from a device where the person was already signed in, and keep it to preserving memories.
  • A memorialised account keeps the photos visible, which is why many families choose it over removal.

Route One: Memorialisation

A memorialised Instagram shows "Remembering" on the profile, freezes it exactly as the person left it, stops it appearing in suggestions and likes, and prevents anyone logging in. Any friend or family member can request it.

1
Copy the profile link

The address looks like instagram.com/username. If the account is private, a family member who follows it can confirm the exact username.

2
Submit the memorialisation form

Search Instagram's Help Centre for "memorialization request". Attach proof of death: a death certificate, an obituary, or a news article naming the person. You do not need to be next of kin for this route.

3
Allow a few weeks and check the profile

Meta often memorialises without sending a confirmation email; the profile simply changes. Resubmit after two weeks of silence.

Route Two: Full Removal

Removal deletes the account and everything in it, permanently. Meta restricts it to immediate family or the estate's legal representative, with documents.

1
Gather the document set

The death certificate, plus proof you have authority: a birth or marriage certificate showing immediate family, or the estate document for your country, such as a grant of probate or letters of administration (UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand) or letters testamentary (US).

2
Submit the removal request

Instagram's removal form for deceased users is at instagram.com/help/contact/1474899482730688. Select removal, provide the username and profile link, and upload clear scans of the documents.

3
Answer follow-ups in the same thread

Meta replies by email and sometimes asks for more evidence of the relationship. Attach everything again each time you reply; requests that make the reviewer search a thread go slower.

Common Problems

Silence

The most common outcome of a first submission is nothing. Resubmit every two weeks with the same documents. Meta processes these in high volume and resubmission genuinely restarts attention.

Fan pages, tributes, and impersonation

After a death, copycat accounts sometimes appear using the person's photos, occasionally messaging relatives or asking followers for money. Report those through Instagram's impersonation reporting, which moves faster than bereavement requests and needs no estate documents.

The Facebook account is linked

Instagram and Facebook accounts stay separate even when linked in Meta's Accounts Centre. Handling one does nothing to the other, so submit both requests. Our Facebook guide covers that side.

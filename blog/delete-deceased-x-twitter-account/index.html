How to Deactivate a Deceased Person's X (Twitter) Account

By Departed Digital · 6 min read · Updated August 2026

X, the platform most people still call Twitter, takes a narrower approach to death than Facebook or LinkedIn. There is no memorial option: an account either stays live exactly as the person left it, or it is deactivated and eventually deleted. For families, the choice is simpler but the process has its own quirks, including a second verification step that catches many people out.

This guide walks through the process as it works today, what X will ask you for, and what X will not do, so you know the limits before you start.

What X Will and Will Not Do

  • X will deactivate the account of a deceased person when an immediate family member or a person authorised to act for the estate requests it with documents.
  • X will not memorialise the account. There is no "Remembering" state as on Facebook or TikTok.
  • X will not give anyone login access to the account, regardless of relationship or court documents.
  • X will not normally provide an archive of the person's posts to the family, so save anything that matters before requesting deactivation.

If the family wants the posts preserved, screenshot or save them first, or use a public web archive to capture the profile as it stands. Once deactivation goes through, the content becomes unreachable and is permanently deleted after a holding period of around 30 days.

Step by Step: Requesting Deactivation

1
Note the username

You need the person's exact handle, the name beginning with @. Copy the profile link too, from the share option on the profile page.

2
Submit the deceased user form

In the X Help Centre, search "deceased" and open the form for reporting a deceased or incapacitated person's account. Fill in your details, your relationship to the person, and the account information. You do not need an X account of your own.

3
Wait for the follow-up email

This is the step people miss. After the form, X emails you asking for more information: a copy of the death certificate, a copy of your own government-issued ID, and sometimes a brief statement of your authority to act. The request lands in the inbox you gave on the form, and occasionally in spam, so watch for it.

4
Send the documents and confirm

Reply with clear scans or photos. X reviews and deactivates the account, then deletes it permanently after the holding period. Expect the whole process to take from a couple of weeks to a couple of months depending on their queue.

Common Problems

No follow-up email arrives

Check spam first. If there is genuinely nothing after a week, submit the form again. Cases go quiet in X's support queue more often than on other platforms, and a fresh submission usually restarts movement.

The account is being misused

If the account posts anything after the person's death, it has been compromised. Report it through X's hacked account and impersonation routes as well as the deceased user form, and say clearly in both that the account holder is deceased. Compromised-account reports move faster than bereavement requests.

The person used X for their business

If the handle carried a business or public following, think before deactivating: the name becomes unavailable, and after deletion the handle can eventually be re-registered by a stranger. Some estates choose to leave a final pinned message and close the account later, or to keep the handle registered to protect the person's name. This is one of the few situations where waiting is a legitimate choice.

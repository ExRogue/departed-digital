How to Close a Deceased Person's TikTok Account

By Steven Kong · 6 min read · Updated October 2026

TikTok is often the platform families feel least equipped to deal with. It now publishes a policy for accounts after a death: it will memorialise or delete an account when the family asks, and it follows the person's own wishes if they chose a legacy contact. The request itself still goes through a general support form and involves some waiting. Meanwhile the account keeps appearing in feeds, keeps gaining followers or comments, and in painful cases keeps being duetted or stitched by strangers.

This guide explains what TikTok will do, what it asks the family for, how its legacy contact works, and one important step to take before the account disappears. It was checked against TikTok's own help page, Managing TikTok accounts after death, in October 2026.

Save the Videos First

Once TikTok deletes an account, the videos go with it. The only download TikTok offers after a death is to a legacy contact the person chose and gave that permission to. For everyone else, if any of the videos matter to the family, save them before you report the death:

  • Public videos can usually be saved from the app using the Save video option on each one, unless the person had downloads disabled.
  • Where downloads are disabled, a screen recording from any phone preserves the video and sound.
  • If a family member knows the account login, the account's own data, including private videos and drafts on the device, can be backed up before any request is made.

This step feels heavy for many families. But do not skip it: deletion is permanent. A memorialised account keeps its videos visible, which is why some families choose that instead.

What TikTok Does With a Deceased Person's Account

TikTok's policy is that when an account owner has died, the account can be memorialised or deleted. TikTok says this usually starts with a request from a family member or legal guardian, that it may memorialise an account without being asked when a death is confirmed through credible sources, and that if the person named a legacy contact, it honours their choice. Be explicit in your request about which outcome the family wants.

A memorialised account, in TikTok's words, stays visible and searchable, and keeps its existing videos and comments. What changes:

  • Nobody can log in to it, and nothing new can be posted.
  • It is no longer recommended to others and does not appear in the For You feed.
  • Other people can no longer Duet or Stitch with its videos.
  • It cannot receive new direct messages, and the message history is not accessible.
  • The profile cannot be edited, unless a legacy contact was given that permission.

Without any report, the account may simply stay live, which is why acting matters: dormant accounts of people who have died are targets for hacking and impersonation.

If They Chose a Legacy Contact

TikTok lets an adult user name one legacy contact, who has to be a TikTok friend, meaning someone they follow who follows them back. The person can also choose to have the account deleted after their death instead. If your loved one used TikTok a lot, ask their close friends whether TikTok has notified any of them: a legacy contact is only told once the account has been memorialised.

What the legacy contact can do depends on what the person allowed. With the profile permission they can update the bio, accept friend requests, manage comments and mentions, and ask for the account to be deleted. With the download permission they can download the person's posts, including private ones if the person chose to include them. A legacy contact cannot log in to the account, edit or remove posts, or read direct messages.

To set one up for yourself: in the TikTok app go to Profile, Menu, Settings and privacy, Account, then Account legacy. Our guide to planning a digital legacy covers the same setting on the other platforms.

How to Report a Death to TikTok: Step by Step

1
Gather the account details

You need the exact username (the handle beginning with @) and a link to the profile. Open the profile in the app or at tiktok.com and copy the link from the share menu.

2
Prepare the documents

TikTok asks for three things: your relationship to the person, proof of death, and proof that the account was theirs. A death certificate is the core document. Add proof of your relationship, such as a birth or marriage certificate, or the document naming you as executor or administrator of the estate. TikTok does not spell out what counts as proof of ownership, so send whatever ties the person to the profile: the profile link with their name and photos on it, or a screenshot of a message they sent you from it.

3
Submit through TikTok's support form

TikTok's help page sends deletion and memorialisation requests to its feedback form at tiktok.com/legal/report/feedback. Choose the closest topic to account issues. State clearly that the account holder has died, give the username and profile link, say whether the family wants memorialisation or deletion, and attach the documents.

4
Report the profile in the app as well

On the profile, tap the share arrow, then Report. This creates a second signal inside TikTok's moderation system. On its own it rarely resolves the case, but combined with the support form it speeds things up.

5
Follow up until it is resolved

TikTok's replies can take weeks and sometimes ask for documents you have already sent. Reply to the same email thread, attach everything again, and restate the request. Persistence is normally what closes these cases.

Common Problems

No reply at all

Resubmit the form after two weeks, and vary the topic selection if the first choice went nowhere. Requests written in clear, short sentences with documents attached from the start get faster treatment than long emotional accounts, hard as that is to write in grief.

The account is being impersonated or misused

If someone has taken over the account, or a copycat account is using the person's videos, report it as impersonation instead. Impersonation reports are handled by a faster queue than bereavement requests on most platforms, TikTok included.

You cannot find the account

Search the username from another family member's account, or check the Following list of a close friend. Private accounts are harder to locate from outside; a mutual follower can usually supply the exact handle.

What Happens to a WhatsApp Account When Someone Dies?

By Steven Kong · 7 min read · Updated October 2026

WhatsApp is often the account that carries the most, and the one with the least protection. The last messages a family exchanged usually live there, yet WhatsApp offers no memorial option, no legacy contact, and no dedicated form for bereaved families. Instead there is a quiet countdown most people have never heard of: an account that stays offline long enough is deleted automatically, and everything visible about it goes too.

This guide explains the countdown, what to save before doing anything else, both routes to deleting the account, and a surprise involving the phone number itself that catches families months later.

Why WhatsApp Is Different

Two design decisions shape everything families can and cannot do.

First, WhatsApp messages are encrypted from end to end, which means they exist on the person's phone, not in an account someone can open from elsewhere. WhatsApp itself cannot read them and cannot hand them over. If the chats matter to your family, the phone is the only place they live, apart from any backups the person set up to their own Google Drive or iCloud.

Second, WhatsApp accounts are tied to a phone number, not an email address. That connects the account's fate to something families often deal with separately and quickly: the mobile contract. More on that below, because the two decisions interact in an unpleasant way.

The Inactivity Clock: 120 Days, or 365 in the US

WhatsApp automatically deletes accounts that stop connecting to the service. Its help pages say accounts are "generally deleted after 120 days of inactivity", and give 365 days for the United States. Inactivity means the account has not connected to WhatsApp at all, so a phone that is switched off, out of battery in a drawer, or wiped counts as disconnected, while a phone left on and online does not. Once the deletion happens, the profile photo, name, groups membership, and the account itself are gone, and nothing brings them back.

For some families this is welcome: do nothing, and the account tidies itself away in about four months, or a year in the US. But the same clock is running on the chats' visibility to you, because if the phone is wiped, lost, or locked forever before anything is exported, the messages go with it. Decide what you want to keep before the practicalities decide for you.

Save the Chats First

If you have access to the unlocked phone, do this before anything else, and certainly before cancelling the mobile contract or deleting the account.

1
Export the conversations that matter

Open a chat, then use the export option (on most phones: the chat's menu → More → Export chat). Choose "Include media" for the conversations you most want to keep. The export arrives as a file you can email to yourself or save to a shared drive.

2
Save the photos and videos

Media received in WhatsApp is usually also in the phone's gallery or Photos app. Back the whole gallery up to a family member's computer or cloud storage, not just the WhatsApp exports.

3
Note the voice notes

For many families the voice notes are the most precious thing on the phone: a voice, saved. They are included when you export a chat with media, but long chats sometimes truncate, so save especially important voice notes individually by sharing them out of the chat.

No access to the phone? The messages cannot be retrieved from WhatsApp itself; encryption means they were never stored where WhatsApp can read them. If the person backed up to Google Drive or iCloud, those backups belong to their Google or Apple account, and our Google and Apple guides explain what is and is not reachable.

Deleting the Account, With the Phone

If you have the unlocked phone, deletion takes two minutes and needs no documents. In WhatsApp, go to Settings → Account → Delete my account, enter the person's phone number in full international format, and confirm. It has to be done on the phone itself, not from WhatsApp Web or a linked tablet. WhatsApp says this erases the message history on the phone, removes the account from all its groups, and deletes its backup.

Only do this after the exports above, because deletion is permanent.

Deleting the Account, Without the Phone

Without the unlocked phone there is no form to fill in. WhatsApp's own help pages say it "can't deactivate accounts for others", because it has no way to check who owns a phone number, so the death certificate and estate documents that work with other platforms are not something WhatsApp asks for. Two routes remain.

1
Use the SIM, if the family has it

WhatsApp identifies an account by its phone number and checks it with a six-digit code sent by text. With the SIM in another phone, the number can be registered on WhatsApp there and the account deleted from Settings. If the SIM is lost or was an eSIM, the phone network's bereavement team can put the number on a new one for whoever is taking the account over.

2
Or let the account lapse

Leave the account unconnected and WhatsApp deletes it itself, generally after 120 days, or 365 days in the US. Make sure the person's phone is switched off or offline, because a phone that is still connecting keeps the account alive.

Each route has catches: a two-step verification PIN, a phone still online in a drawer, a number that has already been cancelled. Our page on deleting a WhatsApp account without the phone takes each situation in turn.

The Phone Number Surprise

This is the part families are never warned about. When a mobile contract is cancelled, the number is not retired; after a waiting period, the carrier reissues it to a stranger. If the WhatsApp account is still live at that point and the new owner of the number installs WhatsApp, they register with that number, and the old account's presence effectively ends there, but the transition can be jarring: the number your family has saved as "Mum" is now an active WhatsApp user who is not her.

The new owner cannot read the old messages. WhatsApp says someone who registers an account on a new device cannot read its past conversations, which stay on the phones of the people who were in them.

The fix is sequencing. Delete the WhatsApp account before, or soon after, cancelling the mobile contract, and tell close family and friends to expect the number to belong to someone new eventually, so nobody messages it months later and gets a reply.

Groups, Profile Photos, and "Last Seen"

Until the account is deleted, it looks unsettlingly alive. The profile photo stays. The name still appears in every group, and group members may keep seeing the person listed for months. Nobody can post as them, but nothing signals that they have died either. If a group matters to the family, a short message from a relative in that group usually settles it better than the platform ever could. Once the account is deleted, the person's name in old group member lists shows as the bare phone number.

Which apps and platforms let you plan for your own death? 20 services compared

By Steven Kong · 9 min read · Updated September 2026

Every platform will tell you what happens to your account when you die, if you know where to look. Almost nobody looks, and families find out the hard way: a phone that cannot be unlocked, a photo library behind a court order, a Snapchat that nobody can delete because deletion needs the person's own email.

So we checked. In September 2026 we read the published bereavement and legacy policies of 20 services people in the UK use every day and answered three questions for each: can you set anything up now so someone can act after your death, what can your family do if you did not, and what does the platform want to see.

What we found. Six of the twenty let you plan ahead: Apple, Google, Facebook, Instagram, Microsoft's OneDrive, and the password managers. Three will not release a person's data to a UK family without a court order if nothing was set up: Apple, Google and Dropbox. One, Snapchat, cannot be closed by a family at all without access to the person's own email. Two delete accounts on a timer whether or not anyone asks: WhatsApp after 120 days, Microsoft after two years. Nobody, anywhere, gives out a password.

"Legacy setting" means something you can switch on while alive so that a named person can act. "Without it" is what a family can do using the platform's bereavement process alone. Four things that stand out

The photos are the problem. The two places most people's photos live, Apple and Google, are also the two that require a court order without a legacy setting. Setting up Apple's Legacy Contact and Google's Inactive Account Manager takes ten minutes between them and is the single most useful thing on this page.

Memorials are a Meta thing. Only Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok keep a profile as a memorial. Everyone else deletes or does nothing. If a family wants somewhere to remember someone, it will be one of those four.

Some accounts cannot be closed from outside. Snapchat's process needs the account's own email. Steam has no process at all. WhatsApp's only route is the phone. For these, the phone and the email are the keys, which is why we tell families never to wipe the phone first.

The clocks are running. WhatsApp deletes after 120 days without a connection. Microsoft freezes a mailbox after a year and closes the account after two. Meta gives 180 days to appeal a suspension. A family that waits for probate before doing anything can lose things that were still there at the funeral.

What to do with this

For yourself: turn on the six that exist. Apple Legacy Contact, Google Inactive Account Manager, a legacy contact or delete-after-death choice on Facebook and Instagram, OneDrive digital legacy if you use it, and emergency access in a password manager. Then write down what you have. Our guide to planning a digital legacy does it in an afternoon.

For someone who has died: start with the phone, then the checklist, and use the platform guides linked in the table. If a request is not working, we have pages for Facebook, Instagram and Apple.

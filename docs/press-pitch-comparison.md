# Press pitch: "Which apps and platforms let you plan for your own death?"

Written 21 September 2026. The asset is https://www.departed.digital/blog/which-platforms-let-you-plan-for-death, a checked comparison of 20 services. Journalists may reproduce the table with a link. Steven Kong is available for comment at hello@departed.digital.

## The story in one paragraph

Most people's photos now live in two accounts, Apple and Google, and both require a court order before they will hand a dead person's data to a UK family unless a legacy setting was switched on first. We checked the published bereavement policies of 20 services people use every day. Six let you plan ahead, three demand a court order without it, one (Snapchat) cannot be closed by a family at all without the person's own email, and two delete accounts on a timer whether anyone asks or not: WhatsApp after 120 days, Microsoft after two years. Nobody gives out a password. Setting up the six takes about half an hour, and almost nobody has.

## Three angles

1. **Consumer, practical:** "The 30 minute job that stops your family needing a court order for your photos." The six settings, step by step, with the table.
2. **Human:** the families on public forums who found out the hard way: seven live phone contracts months after a funeral, a sister's Instagram run by a stranger, the photos on a locked phone. Our stories page has eight, names removed.
3. **Policy:** UK law gives a family no right to a dead person's accounts; each platform makes its own rules and the rules differ wildly. The Law Commission has looked at digital assets; nothing has changed for families yet.

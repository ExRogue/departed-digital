Funeral director call sheet

16 warm leads, 15 calls
Numbers checked on each firm's own website, 14 September 2026
The only goal

Permission to send the card and the 26 second film, and the name and email of the right person. Not a yes to the partnership.

When to call

Mid-morning or mid-afternoon on a weekday. Avoid Monday mornings, when the weekend's arrangements come in.

Mind the line

Most of these numbers are also the firm's 24 hour line for families. Keep it short, and if they sound busy or are with a family, apologise and ask when is better.

The opener

Say this

Hello, is that [firm]? My name's Steven Kong, I run a small London service called Departed Digital. I'm not selling anything today, and I'll be quick.

Families keep getting stuck on the online accounts after a funeral: the Facebook, the email, the Apple account. We close or memorialise those for them. Could I send you a card and a 26 second film, so you can see what it is?

The first family you pass on is free, so you can judge the work before your name goes anywhere near it.

Then: Who's the best person to send it to, and what's their email?

If they ask how it works (30 seconds)

The family tells us which accounts, and sends the death certificate once. We use each platform's own process for a death, chase every one, and give the family a written record at the end. We never ask for passwords. Families pay us directly, from £149, so there's nothing for your team to do. If a family goes ahead we pay you £50, but you don't have to take it.

Use their own page

Each card overleaf has one true thing about the firm. Mention it early: "I was reading your bereavement support page, and it's better than most, but like every aftercare page I've read it stops short of the online accounts."

If it goes to voicemail (office hours only)

"Hi, it's Steven Kong from Departed Digital. I help families close a loved one's online accounts after a funeral. I'll send a short email with a 26 second film. No need to call back. Thank you." Then send the email below, and try once more in a few days.

What they'll say

Four objections, then the quick ones

1. "How many families have you done this for?"

Or: "We've never heard of you."

"We're new, and I'd rather be straight with you about that. It's exactly why the first family you pass on is free. You'll see the whole thing, the updates and the written record at the end, before you decide whether to recommend us to anyone else."

2. "We don't take referral fees."

Or: "It doesn't feel right taking money for that."

"That's completely fine, and plenty of firms feel the same. You don't have to take the fee, and the card works exactly the same without it. The main thing is that families have somewhere to go when the Facebook becomes a problem."

3. "Families can do that themselves."

Or: "We already point them to Cruse and SAIFCare."

"They can, and our do-it-yourself guides are free on the site, so most families start there. The ones who come to us have been stuck in Facebook's forms for weeks, or have five or six accounts and can't face it. This just gives them a next step when that happens. It sits alongside Cruse and SAIFCare, it doesn't replace anything."

4. "Is it safe? What about their data?"

Or: "Do you need their passwords?"

"We never ask for passwords, and we never log into anyone's account. We only use each platform's own process for a death, with the death certificate and proof of who's dealing with the estate. Documents go through a private page for that family only, and everything is handled under UK data protection law."

The quick ones

"Just send me an email." That's a win. "Of course. Who's the best person, and what's their email?"
"What does it cost the family?" "From £149 for up to two accounts, £249 for up to five, £399 for everything."
"I'll need to ask head office." "Of course. I'll send it to you so it's easy to pass on."
"We're not interested." "No problem at all, thank you for your time." Log it and move on.
